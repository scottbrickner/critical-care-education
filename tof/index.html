Counting the Twitch.

Four pulses, one thumb. The count tells you about the drug only if you trust the setup.

A working reference for ICU nurses caring for patients on a neuromuscular blocking agent (NMBA). Get the patient ready, place the electrodes, find the right setting, read the count, and fix a 0/4 before you touch the drip.

Before the drug
01

Before the first twitch

CORE

Paralysis doesn't sedate, numb, or erase memory. A paralyzed patient can be wide awake and in pain, and can't show you.

1

Sedation & analgesia first

Started before the NMBA and kept running the whole time it's on. Never let it lapse.

2

Artificial airway

Every patient on an NMBA has one. No exceptions.

3

Vent rate set ≥ 8

They can't take a breath on their own, so the ventilator needs a backup rate.

4

Eye protection

Artificial tears and lubricant ordered. Lids closed. Corneal injury starts within the first 48 h.

5

Baseline TOF

Find the stimulator setting before the infusion starts. You can't find it afterward. See 03.

SCU10-505 Items 1–5 are written policy requirements.

Your move

HR, BP, sweating and tearing are unreliable signs of pain under paralysis (SCCM 2016), but a sudden change is still a reason to check the sedation and analgesia infusions and call. Talk to the patient during care; they may remember it.

Care of the paralyzed patient

  • Frequent oral care and suctioning
  • DVT and GI prophylaxis, as ordered
  • Turns, skin checks, foot-drop prevention
  • PNS site cleaned and electrodes changed daily
  • Plan positioning and range of motion with PT
  • Watch for unplanned extubation: secure the tube

Things that change the count

  • Electrolyte imbalances, BUN and creatinine can potentiate NMBAs: check baseline labs
  • Edema, hypothermia, sweating: weaker conduction
  • Hemiplegia or neuropathy: that limb doesn't represent the rest of the body
  • A different site or a different device
Deeper · SCCM 2026 & 2016

In ARDS, an NMBA is suggested when the P/F ratio is under 150 and the patient stays hypoxemic or misses ventilation targets despite sedation. Duration usually ≤ 48 h. SCCM suggests against using TOF alone: pair the count with the clinical picture (vent triggering, coughing, movement). A patient at 0/4 can still cough or trigger the vent.

Setup
02

Electrode placement

CORE

You're stimulating a nerve, not a muscle. Two electrodes along the nerve, close together, then watch the one muscle that nerve drives.

Every site

Clip hair if needed, clean with alcohol, let it dry. Two ECG electrodes along the nerve, as close together as possible. Black (−) to distal, red (+) to proximal. Mark the spots with a marker so the next nurse uses the same site.

Ulnar nerve First choice

  1. Palm up, arm relaxed.
  2. Distal (black) electrode over the ulnar nerve, 1–2 cm above the wrist crease on the pinky side.
  3. Proximal (red) electrode on the same line, as close as possible.
  4. Can't isolate it at the wrist? One electrode on either side of the ulnar groove at the elbow.
WatchThumb adducts (pulls in toward the palm). Rest a fingertip on it.
Don't countFinger movement. That's the forearm muscle firing directly, not the nerve. It's artifact.

Posterior tibial nerve Backup

  1. Electrodes behind the medial malleolus, in the groove between the ankle bone and the Achilles tendon.
  2. Distal (black) lower, proximal (red) about 2 cm above it.
  3. Good when the arms are edematous, lined, or wrapped.
WatchGreat toe plantar flexes (curls down).
Not onA paralyzed or partially paralyzed limb (any site).

Facial nerve Last resort

  1. Distal (black) electrode about 2 cm lateral to the outer corner of the eye.
  2. Proximal (red) electrode just in front of the tragus of the ear.
  3. Use only when the other sites don't work.
WatchTwitch of the eyelid and brow on the same side (orbicularis oculi).
Why lastFacial muscles sit right under the electrodes and can twitch from direct stimulation, so the count can read falsely high.
Black · negative · distalRed · positive · proximalNerve path
Safety

Keep PNS leads away from temporary pacing wires: contact can trigger an arrhythmia. Check the skin under the electrodes every day for irritation or breakdown.

Baseline
03

Finding the threshold

CORE

Find the setting before the drug goes in. After that, a drop in the count should mean the drug, not a weak stimulus.

SCU10-505 · Baseline steps
  1. PNS on, start at setting 1.
  2. Press TOF. Watch the right muscle (thumb, toe, or eyelid).
  3. Step the output up until you see 4 strong twitches.
  4. Go one setting higher. That's your monitoring setting for the whole infusion.
  5. Chart the baseline setting and the nerve site.
Why it works

At a low setting only some of the nerve fibers fire, so the twitches are weak. Turn it up and more fibers fire until all of them do and the twitch stops getting stronger. That's the supramaximal point. The extra step is a margin for changes in skin, edema, and electrode contact over the next shift.

Higher isn't better. Too much current fires the muscle directly (fingers jump) and can cause rhythmic nerve firing. It also hurts more.

Deeper · Lippincott 2026 (mA devices)

On a stimulator that reads in milliamps, Lippincott describes the same idea in numbers: start at 5 mA, go up 5 mA at a time until you get 4 twitches, then keep going 5 mA at a time until the twitches stop getting stronger. The supramaximal setting is where four vigorous twitches first appeared. Same logic as SCU10-505, different dial. SCU10-505 governs at Keck.

Timing

10–15 min

Recheck after a bolus or any rate change. Once stable at goal, check every 4–8 h; Keck charts q4h and PRN.

Fade hides

4/4 ≠ strong

Eyes and fingertips can't feel fade once the TOF ratio is above about 0.4. A full count doesn't mean full strength.

Count + patient

0/4 & coughing?

Trust the patient. Triggering the vent or moving at 0/4 means the count isn't telling the whole story.

At the bedside
05

Troubleshooting 0/4

CORE

A dead battery looks exactly like a deep block. Fix the equipment before you change the drug.

SCU10-505 · In this order
  1. Check the battery in the PNS.
  2. Cleanse the site and change the electrodes.
  3. Reposition the electrodes.
  4. Change the polarity of the leads.
  5. Increase the setting, up to the highest.
  6. Retest another nerve.
  7. Still nothing and no spontaneous movement or vent triggering? Call the provider.
Also check
  • Lead wires snapped on at both ends
  • Gel pads dried out or lifting (edema, sweat, diaphoresis)
  • Same site and setting as the last check (read the chart)
  • The infusion: rate, concentration, recent bolus

Once the device is cleared and it's a true 0/4: RN hold or reduce per order, recheck in 10–15 min, and notify.

Know the limit

A bedside PNS can't confirm the patient is strong enough to breathe on their own or be extubated. That takes a quantitative monitor (TOF ratio ≥ 0.9) and the RT and provider's weaning assessment.

Hands-on
06

Hands-on station

DEEPER

Volunteers only. An awake person always shows 4/4, so this is where you practice the setup: placement, threshold, the right muscle, and fixing faults.

Ground rules · Non-negotiable

TOF mode only. Never tetanic, double-burst, or a single high-current pulse on an awake person: it's painful. Start every test at setting 1. Both electrodes on the same limb (or same side of the face). Leads never cross the chest. Anyone can stop at any time, no reason needed.

Read aloud first

"This stimulator sends four small pulses to a nerve. It feels like tingling or a tap, and your thumb or toe may jump. It isn't dangerous, but it can be uncomfortable. Being a volunteer is completely optional, and you can say stop at any time.

Please sit this one out, no explanation needed, if any of these apply: a pacemaker, ICD, or other implanted stimulator; a nerve injury or neuropathy in that arm or leg; broken skin, a rash, or a recent injury where the pads go; you're pregnant; or you'd simply rather not."

Get a clear verbal yes before placing electrodes. For the facial site, ask again; keep pads off the eyelid and stop if there's any eye discomfort.

Facilitator · Fault cards

Plant one per round while the operator looks away. The operator should find it using the SCU10-505 order.

Fault 1

Dead battery

Swap in a dead battery, or flip one so it doesn't connect.

Found at: step 1, battery check.
Fault 2

Dry electrode

Use an old, dried-out pad, or slip a piece of tape under one pad.

Found at: step 2, cleanse and change electrodes.
Fault 3

Wrong spot

Move the pads to the middle of the forearm.

Clue: fingers twitch, the thumb doesn't. Fix: step 3, reposition.
Fault 4

Leads reversed

Swap black and red.

Clue: weaker twitches at the same setting. Fix: step 4, change polarity.
Fault 5

Lead unsnapped

Leave one lead loose on its snap.

Clue: nothing at any setting. Fix: check connections.
Fault 6

Dial left high

Leave the device at a high setting from the last group.

Catch: operator should reset to 1 before the first pulse. Talk through what too much current does.

SCU10-505 crosswalk

CORE

This page teaches from the policy. Open the policy itself (Keck sign-in) when you need the exact wording.

SCU10-505 · Special Care Units: Monitoring Degree of Neuromuscular Blockade with Peripheral Nerve Stimulation ↗

Who gets monitored

Every patient on an NMBA, with an artificial airway, on the vent at a set rate of 8 or more.

Sedation & eyes

Analgesia and sedation started before the NMBA and continued throughout. Artificial tears and eye lubricant ordered.

Site care & equipment

Site cleaned and electrodes changed daily. PNS disinfected between patients by whoever used it.

Baseline

Setting 1 → up to 4 strong twitches → one higher. Done before the infusion starts.

Documentation

Patient and family education; baseline setting and site; TOF response, site, and setting q4h and PRN unless ordered otherwise.

Reversal

4/4 back, plus head lift, wide eye opening, and tongue protrusion each held more than 5 seconds.
